Push, and Weighted
Recall}\label{sovereign-epistemic-acquisition-conditional-design-for-pull-push-and-weighted-recall}

1 College of Electronic and Information Engineering, Nanjing University

2 Jiangsu Yunhefeng Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China

\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Information systems usually treat active retrieval and passive filtering
as separate problems, while the authority to read, write, and audit the
objective is often left implicit. Sovereign Epistemic Acquisition (SEA)
instead formulates a Sovereign Acquisition Problem (SAP): pull and push
operators share one cognitive budget and are constrained by
user-authorized objective writes and audit trails. The contribution is a
conditional integration, not a new general theory of submodular
optimization, rational inattention, or recall estimation. Four formal
results organize the paper. First, a single update kernel, possibly
based on a misspecified credibility parameter, yields an
expected-realized-value identity; correctly specified Bayesian updating
is the nonnegative special case, and misspecification regret can exceed
that value. Second, population threshold analysis is separated from
finite top-\(m\) selection: the Gaussian proposition-level example
requires \(d'=4.3893\) for 95\% population recall, whereas the exact
finite-pool expectation and its realized variability are computed
separately. Third, end-to-end recall is expressed as weighted expected
omission, with an exact coverage--delivery decomposition
\(m_e=1-\kappa_e\delta_e\) and dependence-robust bounds. Pooled class
coverage is not a uniform proposition-conditional floor, and a
Clopper--Pearson interval for a mean does not identify an infimum.
Fourth, a fixed-capacity subset-choice model gives the correct
zero-temperature top-\(m\) limit, without confusing single-alternative
logit with subset inclusion. Existing pilots are semi-oracle,
retrospective mechanism checks: none establishes a 95\% end-to-end
deployment guarantee or superiority to end-to-end LLM, RAG, or
welfare-recommender baselines. Negative and inconsistent recall/value
results, time leakage, shared corpora, and the absence of human data are
reported explicitly. SEA's defensible contribution is therefore a
testable ownership and accounting framework whose deployment claims
remain contingent on matched, leakage-resistant experiments.

\textbf{Keywords}: epistemic sovereignty; active acquisition;
information filtering; misspecified learning; weighted recall; bounded
agency

\section{Part I Problem, Evidence Scope, and

\subsection{1 Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsubsection{1.1 The budget problem and the ownership
problem}\label{the-budget-problem-and-the-ownership-problem}

A bounded agent cannot consume every candidate. If 45 minutes are
available and each substantive item requires at least four minutes, at
most 11 items can be consumed. In a stylized stream of \(10^4\)
candidates, the discarded fraction is therefore at least

\[1-\frac{45}{4\times 10^4}=0.998875,\]

or 99.8875\% (about 99.89\%). The stream size is an illustrative
scenario, not a measured universal constant; the inequality itself is
arithmetic. Notification and personal-information-management research
documents the practical burden of interruptions and digital
organization, but it does not validate this particular daily count for
every user (Pielot, Church \& de Oliveira, 2014; Bergman \& Whittaker,
2016).

Two design questions follow. First, how should active acquisition
(queries, source exploration, monitoring) and passive filtering compete
for the same limited attention? Second, who may read and change the
objective against which candidates are selected, and who can audit those
changes? The second question is not answered by replacing an engagement
objective with a designer's conception of welfare. User-controlled
recommenders, preference elicitation, contestability, and
value-sensitive design already address important parts of it (Harper et
al., 2015; Jugovac \& Jannach, 2017; Hirsch et al., 2017; Friedman \&
Hendry, 2019).

SEA's narrower proposal is to make these components explicit in one
conditional model:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  pull and push are operators in one Sovereign Acquisition Problem
  (SAP);
\item
  objective parameters have authorized read, write, rollback, and audit
  semantics;
\item
  source coverage is distinguished from ingestion, proposition linking,
  quota allocation, and actual consumption;
\item
  empirical recall is reported for a declared target distribution and
  consumption unit, rather than treated as a context-free percentage.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{1.2 Contributions and
non-contributions}\label{contributions-and-non-contributions}

The paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A behaviorally consistent value model.} A single update kernel
  is used for every observed signal. A reliability-mixture
  specialization separates the true credibility \(c\) from the agent's
  assumed credibility \(\hat c\), without allowing the update rule to
  depend on an unobserved genuine/adversarial branch.
\item
  \textbf{Aligned filtering calculations.} The paper distinguishes
  combinatorial capacity bounds, population threshold models, and finite
  top-\(m\) order statistics. It also corrects the Pareto leakage
  expression by giving the exact Gamma sum and labeling the integral
  expression as an approximation.
\item
  \textbf{A weighted end-to-end recall theory.} The main object is
  weighted expected omission, not a pooled estimate inserted into a
  pointwise redundancy formula. Exact identities separate source
  noncoverage from delivery loss, support dependence-robust bounds, and
  clarify what an independent proposition sample can and cannot
  estimate.
\item
  \textbf{An evidence and validation audit.} The paper reports the
  limits of existing semi-oracle pilots, corrects their denominators and
  budget calibers, decomposes negative citation-weighted returns, and
  specifies the direct baselines and temporal controls required for
  structural identification.
\end{enumerate}

The following are not claimed: a universal need for dual channels; a
five-dimensional sufficient statistic for all decision value; a measured
independence coefficient \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\); a pointwise 95\% or
99.76\% deployment guarantee; a new KKT, Hoeffding, Poisson-binomial,
minimax-regret, or submodular-greedy theorem; or evidence that SEA
improves human welfare.

\subsubsection{1.3 Claim and evidence
conventions}\label{claim-and-evidence-conventions}

A \textbf{formal result} is a derivation under its stated assumptions. A
\textbf{design rule} is a normative or engineering choice. A
\textbf{descriptive pilot} summarizes a frozen retrospective artifact. A
\textbf{confirmatory experiment} would require a time-stamped protocol,
independent environments, matched baselines, and an immutable analysis
package; no such complete experiment is reported here.

The word \emph{recall} always requires four qualifiers: target set,
probability measure or weighting, configuration, and consumption unit.
In the empirical sections it means \textbf{retrospective
proxy-opportunity recall}, with upstream conditioning stated explicitly.
It never abbreviates ``recall of every proposition that would have been
critical ex ante to a person.'' The authoritative evidence matrix
appears once, in Section 9; later discussion interprets rather than
reintroduces competing calibers.

\subsection{2 Related Work and Precise
Positioning}\label{related-work-and-precise-positioning}

\subsubsection{2.1 Review method}\label{review-method}

The literature update uses a targeted scoping search rather than a
systematic review. Search concepts, inclusion and exclusion rules,
access date, and representative publisher records are archived in
\texttt{docs/LITERATURE\_SEARCH\_v1.9.md}. Databases and queries covered
user-controlled recommendation, diversified retrieval and submodular
summarization, misspecified learning, selective prediction and cascade
retrieval, personal information management and notifications, meaningful
human control and contestability, and agenda setting and common
knowledge. Because the search is not exhaustive, the positioning claim
is deliberately limited: among the literature identified by this search,
we have not found one formal integration that simultaneously treats pull
and push under one budget, explicit objective write/audit rights,
end-to-end weighted omission, and joint counterfactual auditing. Each
component has substantial prior work.

\subsubsection{2.2 Acquisition, attention, and value of
information}\label{acquisition-attention-and-value-of-information}

Search economics (Stigler, 1961), optimal stopping and Pandora's box
(Weitzman, 1979), information foraging (Pirolli \& Card, 1999), Bayesian
experimental design (Lindley, 1956; Chaloner \& Verdinelli, 1995),
active learning (Settles, 2009), bandits (Gittins, 1979; Whittle, 1988),
and rational inattention (Sims, 2003; Matějka \& McKay, 2015; Maćkowiak,
Matějka \& Wiederholt, 2023) supply the pull-side foundations. Blackwell
(1953) gives the correctly specified information-value benchmark. SAP
uses these as components of a shared-budget control problem; it does not
imply that their indices have the same units or that a layered solution
is globally optimal.

Selective prediction and the reject option formalize the choice to
abstain when confidence is inadequate (Chow, 1970; Geifman \& El-Yaniv,
2017). Cascade ranking studies how cheap stages can preserve retrieval
quality while reducing expensive computation (Wang, Lin \& Metzler,
2011). Recall@k and relevance-judgment variability are established
retrieval-evaluation concerns (Voorhees, 1998). These literatures
motivate separate reporting of upstream candidate recall, judgment-layer
recall, abstention, and final consumption. SEA adds no new retrieval
metric; it requires these established stages not to be collapsed into
one headline number.

\subsubsection{2.3 User control, welfare, and
contestability}\label{user-control-welfare-and-contestability}

User-controlled recommendation already allows people to alter
recommendation objectives and trade-offs. Harper et al.~(2015), for
example, let users tune popularity and recency controls; Jugovac and
Jannach (2017) review a broader interaction literature. Welfare-oriented
recommenders study objectives other than raw engagement (Allcott et al.,
2020; Stray et al., 2024). It would therefore be inaccurate to claim
that prior research never asked who configures a recommender's
objective.

SEA's narrower distinction is the combination of explicit machine-state
write authorization, reversible change logs, a common pull--push budget,
and an end-to-end actual-consumption estimand. This is adjacent to, not
a replacement for, meaningful human control (Santoni de Sio \& van den
Hoven, 2018), contestability (Hirsch et al., 2017), and value-sensitive
design (Friedman \& Hendry, 2019). These traditions also warn that a
readable interface or a signature alone does not establish meaningful
control.

\subsubsection{2.4 Diversified retrieval and submodular
summarization}\label{diversified-retrieval-and-submodular-summarization}

Maximal marginal relevance (Carbonell \& Goldstein, 1998), xQuAD
(Santos, Macdonald \& Ounis, 2010), PM-2 (Dang \& Croft, 2012), and
submodular document summarization (Lin \& Bilmes, 2011) directly address
relevance, redundancy, and coverage. Facility-location objectives and
classical monotone-submodular guarantees provide further foundations
(Nemhauser, Wolsey \& Fisher, 1978; Krause, Singh \& Guestrin, 2008).

The SEA selection objective is an instance of this family, not an
alternative invented independently of it. Weighted proposition coverage
inherits diminishing returns; facility-location diversity is a standard
submodular term. MMR is a sequential relevance--redundancy heuristic,
xQuAD explicitly models subtopic coverage, and PM-2 targets proportional
aspect representation. SEA differs mainly in its declared ownership
constraints and its requirement to audit actual consumption. Its
approximation ratios, when applicable, are inherited from standard
algorithms and restricted to their certified domains (Leskovec et al.,
2007; Sviridenko, 2004).

\subsubsection{2.5 Misspecified learning and non-Bayesian realized
value}\label{misspecified-learning-and-non-bayesian-realized-value}

The nonnegativity of correctly specified information value does not
cover an agent acting on a wrong likelihood. Misspecified-statistics and
misspecified-learning research studies exactly this divergence (Berk,
1966; Esponda \& Pouzo, 2016; Fudenberg, Romanyuk \& Strack, 2017). SEA
uses this upstream directly: the environment generates signals under
\(c\), while one behaviorally implementable update kernel uses
\(\hat c\) for every signal. The resulting expected realized utility is
not a Blackwell value unless the complete model, including the prior and
posterior, is correctly specified.

\subsubsection{2.6 Human measurement and public
communication}\label{human-measurement-and-public-communication}

Brier scores (Brier, 1950) combine calibration-related reliability,
resolution, and outcome uncertainty (Murphy, 1973; Gneiting \& Raftery,
2007). Strict propriety concerns expected-score minimization under a
specified incentive model; it does not show that every participant in
every scoring environment has no strategic reason to distort a report.
Notification batching and digital-wellbeing research motivates
time-budget interventions but does not validate long-term epistemic
change (Fitz et al., 2019).

Agenda-setting research studies which issues become salient (McCombs \&
Shaw, 1972), while public rituals and common knowledge address the
additional fact that participation is publicly visible (Aumann, 1976;
Chwe, 2001). Public signals may also receive excessive weight in
coordination problems (Morris \& Shin, 2002). These are distinct
mechanisms. A shared digest can change agenda overlap without producing
common knowledge of truth.

\subsection{3 The Sovereign Acquisition Problem and Its Governance
Constraints}\label{the-sovereign-acquisition-problem-and-its-governance-constraints}

\subsubsection{3.1 Items, propositions, and actual
consumption}\label{items-propositions-and-actual-consumption}

An information item \(x_i\) belongs to a source class \(\mathcal C_i\)
and is mapped to one or more propositions \(e\in E\). A proposition is
not automatically a document, a company--day, or an event: the target
construction \(\kappa:x_i\mapsto e\) is part of the estimand and must be
versioned. For a target proposition \(e\) and source class \(i\), let
\(Z_i(e)\) indicate authentic source coverage. Coverage does not imply
that the item was ingested, authenticated, linked correctly, selected,
or actually consumed.

A policy
\(\pi=(\pi_{\mathrm{mon}},\pi_{\mathrm{pull}},\pi_{\mathrm{push}},\pi_{\mathrm{del}},\pi_{\mathrm{esc}})\)
chooses a monitoring portfolio, acquisition operators, push selection,
delivery, and any emergency action. Pull and push costs, reading time
\(T_{\mathrm{push}}\), reflection time \(T_{\mathrm{ref}}\), and
acquisition time \(T_{\mathrm{pull}}\) obey

\[T_{\mathrm{pull}}+T_{\mathrm{push}}+T_{\mathrm{ref}}\le T.\]

The complete objective is a declared expected discounted sum of realized
decision utility, reflection/learning utility, and harm terms. The full
SAP is not solved globally in this paper. In particular,
monotone-submodular selection, independent coverage bounds, and concave
budget allocation address different restricted subproblems and do not
compose automatically into a global approximation ratio.

\subsubsection{3.2 A valid finite-horizon existence
statement}\label{a-valid-finite-horizon-existence-statement}

The original reduced tuple
\((K_t,\theta_t,\text{budget},\pi_{\mathrm{mon},t})\) is not assumed to
be Markov. Authorization state, the second-order objective
\(g_t^{(2)}\), exposure and queue histories, public and emergency
commitments, current candidate states, and producer/adversary states can
all affect rewards, feasibility, or transitions. Latent
producer/adversary states belong to the transition model, not
automatically to the policy's information set.

\textbf{Proposition 3.1 (Optimal policy for a finite observable-history
SAP).} \emph{Suppose the SAP has finite horizon \(H\) and bounded
one-period rewards (including any reflection term). Primitive actions
and observations have finite range; latent exogenous states also have
finite range so that the model remains discrete. Let
\(h_t=(o_0,a_0,\ldots,a_{t-1},o_t)\) be the observable history,
including every authorization and audit variable available to the
policy. Latent states enter only transition and reward kernels and any
belief formed from \(h_t\); they are not inputs to the policy. Encode
every budget, exposure, tolerance, authorization, audit, public, and
emergency constraint into a nonempty finite feasible set
\(\mathcal A_t(h_t)\). Then an optimal deterministic history-dependent
policy exists.}

\textbf{Proof.} Finite actions and observations generate finitely many
observable histories at each finite time. Set \(V_{H+1}=0\) and
recursively compute

\[V_t(h)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A_t(h)}\left\{r_t(h,a)+\beta\sum_{o_{t+1}}P(o_{t+1}\mid h,a)V_{t+1}(h,a,o_{t+1})\right\}.\]

Every maximum is over a nonempty finite set and is attained. Choosing a
maximizer with a fixed tie rule and concatenating the decisions proves
the result without requiring latent-state knowledge. \(\blacksquare\)

This is not a proof that the reduced tuple is Markov or that a
low-dimensional belief is sufficient. Continuous dwelling times or
budget allocations require a separate Borel-MDP argument and are outside
this proposition. The general SAP also remains computationally
difficult; restless-bandit special cases are already PSPACE-hard
(Papadimitriou \& Tsitsiklis, 1999).

\subsubsection{3.3 Sovereignty as structural constraints, not a
mutual-information
slogan}\label{sovereignty-as-structural-constraints-not-a-mutual-information-slogan}

Let \(\vartheta=(w,g^{(2)},\pi_{\mathrm{mon}},\text{thresholds})\)
denote objective parameters and \(\Psi^{\mathrm{plat}}\) third-party
platform variables. The design requirements are:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{S1, inspectability:} the implemented objective, parameters,
  defaults, and relevant data provenance are readable by the reflective
  principal \(R\).
\item
  \textbf{S2, authorized writes and rollback:} every change to
  \(\vartheta\) requires an authorization record \(\sigma_R\), old and
  new values, a timestamp, and a reversible implementation. Behavioral
  measurements may inform a proposal but cannot silently become a write.
\item
  \textbf{S3, revenue exclusion and counterfactual invariance:}
  \(U_\vartheta\) contains no third-party revenue term and platform
  variables cannot write \(\vartheta\) without \(\sigma_R\). For fixed
  candidate pool \(C\), user state \(\kappa\), and authorized
  \(\vartheta\), the direct platform-side counterfactual satisfies
  \[\operatorname{Rank}_\vartheta(C,\kappa;\psi)=\operatorname{Rank}_\vartheta(C,\kappa;\psi')\]
  for tested \(\psi,\psi'\). The audit also logs unauthorized write
  attempts.
\item
  \textbf{S4, auditability and recourse:} admission, ranking, delivery,
  override, and rollback decisions leave append-only records with
  evidence pointers and a contestation channel.
\end{itemize}

No mutual-information equation is used. \(I(U;\mathrm{Rev})=0\) would be
neither necessary nor sufficient: a degenerate revenue variable can have
zero mutual information even when revenue is an explicit objective term,
while statistical dependence does not by itself identify an unauthorized
causal write. The counterfactual test covers only tested variables and
fixed supply; platform influence through the candidate ecology needs a
separate audit. S1--S4 are therefore design constraints, not proof of

\section{Part II Conditional Formal
Results}\label{part-ii-conditional-formal-results}

\subsection{4 Expected Realized Value under One Misspecified Update
Rule}\label{expected-realized-value-under-one-misspecified-update-rule}

\subsubsection{4.1 Arbitrary update
kernel}\label{arbitrary-update-kernel}

For decision \(d\), let \(P_{i,d}\) be the true joint distribution of
\((\omega_d,Y_i)\), let \(\mu_{i,d}\) be the agent's current prior, and
let \(\widehat\mu_{i,d}(\cdot\mid y)\) be \textbf{one arbitrary
measurable update kernel used for every signal}. Time and deadline
effects are included in \(u^h_{i,d}\). Assume all displayed utilities
are integrable and all required maxima are attained. Define

\[a^0_{i,d}\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_{\mu_{i,d}}[u^h_{i,d}(a,\omega_d)],\]

\[\widehat a_{i,d}(y)\in\arg\max_a\mathbb E_{\widehat\mu_{i,d}(\cdot\mid y)}[u^h_{i,d}(a,\omega_d)].\]

\textbf{Definition 4.1 (Expected realized decision value).}

\[\mathcal W_{i,d}:=\mathbb E_{P_{i,d}}\!\left[u^h_{i,d}(\widehat a_{i,d}(Y_i),\omega_d)-u^h_{i,d}(a^0_{i,d},\omega_d)\right],\qquad v_i^*:=\sum_{d\in D}\pi_d(i)\mathcal W_{i,d}.\]

Only one update kernel appears. No genuine/adversarial branch label is
observed or used to select an update rule. Gains and losses can be
separated without imposing their signs: if \(\Delta U_{i,d}\) is the
utility increment inside the expectation and
\(G_{i,d}=\mathbb E[(\Delta U_{i,d})^+]\),
\(L_{i,d}=\mathbb E[(\Delta U_{i,d})^-]\), then
\(\mathcal W_{i,d}=G_{i,d}-L_{i,d}\).

\subsubsection{4.2 Correctly specified special case and misspecification
regret}\label{correctly-specified-special-case-and-misspecification-regret}

If \(\mu_{i,d}=P_{i,d,\omega}\) and
\(\widehat\mu_{i,d}(\cdot\mid y)=P_{i,d}(\cdot\mid Y_i=y)\), the
standard tower argument gives

\[\mathcal W_{i,d}=\mathbb E_y\!\left[\max_a\mathbb E(u^h_{i,d}\mid y)\right]-\max_a\mathbb E_{\mu_{i,d}}u^h_{i,d}=\mathrm{VOI}_{i,d}\ge0.\]

The complete prior, likelihood, posterior, and evaluation distribution
must agree; a ``correct likelihood'' attached to a wrong prior is not
enough.

For a parametric reliability model, let \(Q_c\) be the true joint law
with prior \(\mu=P_\omega\) and let \(Q_{\hat c}\) be the agent's
assumed law. The agent always uses the posterior induced by
\(Q_{\hat c}\). Let \(a_c^*(y)\) be the Bayes action under \(Q_c\) and
\(\widehat a_{\hat c}(y)\) the action under \(Q_{\hat c}\). Define the
correctly specified value \(\mathrm{VOI}_c\) and realized value
\(W(c,\hat c)\) relative to the same no-signal baseline.

\textbf{Theorem 4.1 (Value minus misspecification regret).} \emph{Under
the integrability and attainment assumptions above,}

\[W(c,\hat c)=\mathrm{VOI}_c-\operatorname{Regret}_c(\hat c),\]

where

\[\operatorname{Regret}_c(\hat c):=\mathbb E_{Q_c}\!\left[u(a_c^*(Y),\omega)-u(\widehat a_{\hat c}(Y),\omega)\right]\ge0.\]

Consequently \(W(c,\hat c)<0\) is possible exactly when the regret
exceeds the correctly specified value.

\textbf{Proof.} Add and subtract \(\mathbb E_{Q_c}u(a_c^*(Y),\omega)\).
The regret is nonnegative because, conditional on each \(y\),
\(a_c^*(y)\) maximizes expected utility under the true posterior
\(Q_c(\omega\mid y)\). \(\blacksquare\)

A reliability-mixture implementation is

\[\ell_{\hat c}(y\mid\omega)=\hat c\,\ell_G(y\mid\omega)+(1-\hat c)\ell_A(y\mid\omega),\]

with the corresponding single posterior
\(\widehat\mu(B\mid y)\propto\int_B\mu(d\omega)\ell_{\hat c}(y\mid\omega)\).
The true environment may use \(c\ne\hat c\), but the update rule never
observes the latent \(G/A\) branch. Credibility mismatch need not cause
harm in every problem---different posteriors can induce the same
actions---but it can cause strictly negative realized value.

\subsubsection{4.3 Binary counterexample}\label{binary-counterexample}

Let \(\omega\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), actions be \(0,1\), and
utility be one for a correct action and zero otherwise. A genuine
mechanism is correct with probability \(\eta>1/2\); an adversarial
mechanism returns the opposite state with the same probability. Under
reliability \(c\), the observed signal is correct with probability

\[a_c=c\eta+(1-c)(1-\eta).\]

Take \(c=0.2\), \(\hat c=0.8\), and \(\eta=0.9\). The agent uses only
\(Q_{0.8}\), perceives signal accuracy \(a_{0.8}=0.74>1/2\), and
therefore follows every signal. The true accuracy is \(a_{0.2}=0.26\),
so \(W=-0.24\). With the correctly specified \(Q_{0.2}\), the agent
reverses the signal and obtains \(\mathrm{VOI}=0.24\). Hence
misspecification regret is \(0.48\). The negative value arises from one
implementable rule applied uniformly to all observations, not from
branch-contingent updating.

\subsubsection{4.4 Status of scoring
features}\label{status-of-scoring-features}

Relevance, novelty, durability, actionability, and credibility can
remain engineering coordinates \(\phi_i\). They are not proved to be a
universal sufficient statistic for value. An exact representation
\(W_i=F(\phi_i;K,D)\) holds only when such a map and its decision
homogeneity assumptions are externally specified and validated. That is
a conditional parameterization, close to a representability
assumption---not evidence that the five semantic names exhaust decision
value.

A useful restricted form is obtained only after additional assumptions.
If \(G_{i,d}=a_dG_i\), \(L_{i,d}=a_dL_i\), and
\(A_i=\sum_d\pi_d(i)a_d\), then \(v_i^*=A_i(G_i-L_i)\). If separately
validated maps \(G_i=\mathcal G(\phi_i)\), \(L_i=\mathcal L(\phi_i)\),
and \(A_i=\mathcal A(\phi_i)\) exist, then

\[v_i^*=\mathcal A(\phi_i)\{\mathcal G(\phi_i)-\mathcal L(\phi_i)\}.\]

Outside those conditions the full update kernel, decision-specific
utilities, and harm/loss terms remain necessary. Linear scoring on the
five coordinates is a heuristic whose error must be measured, not a
decision-theoretic identity.

\subsection{5 Filtering: Capacity, Population Thresholds, and Finite
Selection}\label{filtering-capacity-population-thresholds-and-finite-selection}

\subsubsection{5.1 Capacity and exact Pareto
leakage}\label{capacity-and-exact-pareto-leakage}

\textbf{Proposition 5.1 (Capacity and optimal unavoidable leakage).}
\emph{If \(N\) items arrive, each costs at least \(\underline\tau>0\),
and the budget is \(T\), then at most \(T/\underline\tau\) items can be
consumed. If \(K\) critical propositions have nonnegative values \(v_e\)
and at most \(m\) distinct propositions can be consumed, define
\(\mathcal D(\pi):=\mathbb E[\sum_{e=1}^Kv_e\mathbf 1\{e\text{ is not consumed by }\pi\}]\).
Then}

\[\mathcal D(\pi)\ge\mathcal D^*:=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{r=m_0+1}^{K}V_{(r)}\right],\qquad m_0=\min(m,K),\]

\emph{where \(V_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge V_{(K)}\) is the decreasing
rearrangement. Equality holds exactly when the consumed critical value
equals the top-\(m_0\) critical value almost surely; ties are allowed.}

\textbf{Proof.} The consumed critical value cannot exceed the sum of the
\(m_0\) largest values. A policy attaining that sum reaches equality.
\(\blacksquare\)

For \(v_e\overset{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Pareto}(\alpha,v_{\min})\),
\(\alpha>1\), the exact order-statistic expectation is

\[\mathbb E V_{(r)}=v_{\min}\frac{\Gamma(r-1/\alpha)\Gamma(K+1)}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(K+1-1/\alpha)}.\]

Thus the exact expected aggregate leaked share is

\[\frac{\mathcal D^*}{\mathbb E[\sum_e v_e]}=1-\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{m_0}\mathbb E V_{(r)}}{Kv_{\min}\alpha/(\alpha-1)}.\]

As \(K\to\infty\), \(m\to\infty\), and \(m/K\to\rho\in(0,1)\), the
quantile-integral limit (Resnick, 1987) is

\[\frac{\mathcal D^*}{\mathbb E[\sum_e v_e]}\;\longrightarrow\;1-\rho^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}.\]

At finite \(K,m\), \(1-(m/K)^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}\) is therefore an
approximation, not an equality. At \(K=52,\alpha=1.5\), the
exact/approximate pairs are 20.7999\%/20.6299\% for \(m=26\) and
6.9191\%/6.8716\% for \(m=42\). The latter approximation uses the actual
ratio \(42/52=0.807692\), not 0.8. Direct pass-through does not increase
total consumption capacity; it changes how the same slots are allocated.

\subsubsection{5.2 Population threshold model and boundary
cases}\label{population-threshold-model-and-boundary-cases}

Let noncritical and critical scores have continuous distributions
\(F_0,F_1\) and survival functions \(\bar F_0,\bar F_1\), with critical
mass \(\pi\in(0,1)\). At a fixed threshold \(t\), the population
acceptance and recall are

\[\beta(t)=(1-\pi)\bar F_0(t)+\pi\bar F_1(t),\qquad R(t)=\bar F_1(t).\]

This is a population threshold identity. It is not an exact identity for
the random threshold induced by finite top-\(m\) selection.

\textbf{Theorem 5.2 (Threshold feasibility).} \emph{For \(0<R<1\) and
\(0<\beta<1\), a counting-necessary condition is \(\beta\ge\pi R\). Let
\(\tau_R=Q_1(1-R)\), where \(Q_1\) is the ordinary CDF quantile.
Assuming the survival functions are continuous and strictly decreasing
on their relevant supports, a threshold with acceptance \(\beta\) and
recall at least \(R\) exists if and only if}

\[\bar F_0(\tau_R)\le b,\qquad b:=\frac{\beta-\pi R}{1-\pi}.\]

\emph{For equal-variance Gaussian scores separated by \(d'\ge0\), the
complete boundary-aware minimum is}

\[\underline d'=\begin{cases}
\text{infeasible},&b<0,\\
+\infty,&b=0,\\
\max\{0,\Phi^{-1}(1-b)+\Phi^{-1}(R)\},&0<b<1,\\
0,&b\ge1.
\end{cases}\]

\textbf{Proof.} At \(\tau_R\), critical recall is \(R\). If the
displayed inequality holds, total acceptance there is at most \(\beta\).
Because \(\beta<1\), lowering the threshold continuously fills the
budget and cannot reduce critical recall. Conversely, any threshold with
recall at least \(R\) must satisfy \(t\le\tau_R\), so the noncritical
contribution is at least \(\bar F_0(\tau_R)\); budget feasibility
requires the inequality. For \(0<b<1\), the Gaussian expression follows
from \(z=\Phi^{-1}(1-b)\) and \(z-d'=-\Phi^{-1}(R)\). When \(b\ge1\),
the threshold condition is automatic and identical score distributions
(\(d'=0\)) already give recall \(\beta\ge R\). \(\blacksquare\)

The remaining boundaries are separate. If \(\beta<\pi R\) (equivalently
\(b<0\)), the target is impossible even for perfect ranking. For a
nondegenerate finite Gaussian \(d'\), \(b=0\) (equivalently
\(\beta=\pi R\)) requires infinite separation when \(R>0\). Likewise
\(R=1\) with \(\beta<1\) requires infinite separation; \(\beta=1\) is
handled outside the theorem as the trivial consume-everything rule with
recall one for every \(d'\). If \(Q_1\) is a CDF quantile, 95\% critical
recall uses \(Q_1(0.05)\), not \(Q_1(0.95)\).

At the \textbf{proposition-level} parameters
\(\beta=0.0125,\pi=0.01,R=0.95\),

\[\underline d'=4.389337888,\qquad \mathrm{AUC}=\Phi(\underline d'/\sqrt2)=0.999044514.\]

Population recalls at \(d'=1.5\) and \(2.5\) are 0.209639680 and
0.523766570. By contrast, the illustrative document-level parameter
\(\beta=0.001\) gives \(R\le\beta/\pi=0.1\), so 95\% is
capacity-infeasible irrespective of separation. These are model
calculations, not evidence that dual channels are necessary for every
retrieval architecture.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{5.3 Finite top-\(m\): exact formula
evaluated by
quadrature}{5.3 Finite top-m: exact formula evaluated by quadrature}}\label{finite-top-m-exact-formula-evaluated-by-quadrature}

Let \(M\) be finite. All \(M\) scores are mutually independent, with
\(K\) critical scores drawn from \(F_1\) and \(M-K\) noncritical scores
drawn from \(F_0\). Select exactly the \(m\) largest scores,
\(1\le m<M\). The threshold is an order statistic, not the population
threshold above.

\textbf{Proposition 5.2 (Finite-pool expected recall).} \emph{For
continuous scores and \(K\ge1\), symmetry among critical items gives}

\[\mathbb E R_{M,K,m}=\int f_1(x)\Pr\{N_0(x)+N_1(x)\le m-1\}\,dx,\]

\emph{where, conditional on one marked critical score \(x\),}

\[N_0(x)\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(M-K,\bar F_0(x)),\qquad N_1(x)\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(K-1,\bar F_1(x)),\]

\emph{independently. The marked critical item is selected exactly when
at most \(m-1\) other scores exceed it.}

\subsubsection{5.4 Coverage--diversity objectives: inherited guarantees
and their
limits}\label{coveragediversity-objectives-inherited-guarantees-and-their-limits}

For candidate set \(A\), consider

\[U(A)=\sum_{e\in E}v_e\mathbf 1\{A\cap D_e\ne\varnothing\}+\mu\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal S}\max_{i\in A}\operatorname{sim}(\sigma,i)-\lambda\sum_{i\in A}\hat m_i\tau_i,\]

with nonnegative values, similarities, costs, and weights. The first
term is weighted coverage and the second is facility-location diversity.
Both are nonnegative, monotone, and submodular. The complete penalized
objective remains submodular but need not be monotone.

Consequently, the usual cardinality ratio \(1-1/e\) applies to the
unpenalized objective or to a separately certified monotone restriction,
not automatically to the full pool. One sufficient restriction
\(\mathcal C_M\) requires, for every \(i\in\mathcal C_M\), the smallest
positive-part coverage/diversity gain over all subcontexts to dominate
\(\lambda\hat m_i\tau_i\). All optima and ratios are then relative to
that restricted ground set. Under positive unequal costs, density greedy
plus the best feasible singleton has the standard \(\tfrac12(1-1/e)\)
guarantee, and depth-three partial enumeration has the standard
\(1-1/e\) guarantee (Leskovec et al., 2007; Sviridenko, 2004).
Unaugmented density greedy is not assigned either ratio.

This is the same algorithmic lineage as diversified retrieval and
submodular summarization. The engineering contribution is the audit
requirement to log monotonicity certificates, algorithm variant,
residual capacities, and restricted optimum---not a new approximation
theorem.

\subsubsection{5.5 Online thresholds with explicit
boundaries}\label{online-thresholds-with-explicit-boundaries}

Suppose an item arrives independently with probability \(a\in(0,1]\) in
each period; conditional on arrival, its reward \(R=r(S)\) is iid with
\(\mathbb E R^+<\infty\) and \(r\) strictly increasing. At most \(b\)
items can be consumed over the remaining periods and discarding is
irrevocable. Let \(W_i(b)\) be optimal continuation value and
\(\Delta_i(b)=W_i(b)-W_i(b-1)\).

\textbf{Proposition 5.3 (Threshold policy).} \emph{With
\(W_i(0)=W_0(b)=0\) and \(g_a(\tau)=a\mathbb E[(R-\tau)^+]\),}

\[W_i(b)=W_{i-1}(b)+g_a(\Delta_{i-1}(b)).\]

\emph{It is strictly optimal to consume when \(r(s)>\Delta_{i-1}(b)\)
and not optimal when \(r(s)<\Delta_{i-1}(b)\); either action is optimal
on ties. Moreover \(\Delta_i(b)\) is nonnegative, nonincreasing in
\(b\), and nondecreasing in \(i\).}

\textbf{Proof.} The Bellman equation follows by comparing consumption
and discard. Subtracting \(W_i(0)=0\) gives the distinct boundary
recursion

\[\Delta_i(1)=\Delta_{i-1}(1)+g_a(\Delta_{i-1}(1)),\]

whereas for \(b\ge2\),

\[\Delta_i(b)=\Delta_{i-1}(b)+g_a(\Delta_{i-1}(b))-g_a(\Delta_{i-1}(b-1)).\]

The function \(g_a\) is nonnegative, nonincreasing, and \(a\)-Lipschitz
with \(a\le1\). I prove concavity by induction, with base
\(\Delta_0(b)=0\) for \(b\ge1\). At \(b=2\), write
\(y=\Delta_{i-1}(1)\ge z=\Delta_{i-1}(2)\). Then

\[\Delta_i(1)-\Delta_i(2)=(y-z)+g_a(y)+[g_a(y)-g_a(z)]\ge0.\]

For every \(b\ge3\), write
\(x=\Delta_{i-1}(b-2)\ge y=\Delta_{i-1}(b-1)\ge z=\Delta_{i-1}(b)\).
Then

\[\Delta_i(b-1)-\Delta_i(b)=(y-z)+[g_a(y)-g_a(x)]+[g_a(y)-g_a(z)]\ge0,\]

because \(g_a(y)-g_a(x)\ge0\) and \((y-z)+[g_a(y)-g_a(z)]\ge0\) by the
Lipschitz property. Nonnegativity and time monotonicity follow
separately: at \(b=1\),
\(\Delta_i(1)=\Delta_{i-1}(1)+g_a(\Delta_{i-1}(1))\ge\Delta_{i-1}(1)\);
at \(b\ge2\), the induction hypothesis
\(\Delta_{i-1}(b)\le\Delta_{i-1}(b-1)\) and monotonicity of \(-g_a\)
give \(\Delta_i(b)\ge\Delta_{i-1}(b)\). \(\blacksquare\)

A finite cooling queue introduces occupancy, age, eviction, and decay
states. The present proof does not establish a double-threshold queue
policy, and no such structural claim is made here.

\subsection{6 Recall as Weighted Expected
Omission}\label{recall-as-weighted-expected-omission}

\subsubsection{6.1 Separate source coverage from end-to-end
delivery}\label{separate-source-coverage-from-end-to-end-delivery}

Fix a monitoring set \(S\) and proposition \(e\). Let \(Z_i(e)\) denote
authentic coverage by source class \(i\), with \(p_i(e)=P(Z_i(e)=1)\),
and define

\[C_S(e)=\bigcup_{i\in S}Z_i(e),\qquad \kappa_e(S)=P(C_S(e)).\]

Let \(D_S(e)\) be actual consumption of \(e\) through the monitored
channel, with \(D_S(e)\subseteq C_S(e)\). When \(\kappa_e(S)>0\), define
the complete delivery conversion probability

\[\delta_e(S):=P(D_S(e)\mid C_S(e)).\]

It includes ingestion, authentication, proposition linking,
deduplication, quota allocation, selection, and actual consumption. When
\(\kappa_e(S)=0\), actual delivery is zero and \(\delta_e\) need not be
defined; the convention \(\kappa_e(S)\delta_e(S)=0\) is used in the
identities below.

\textbf{Theorem 6.1 (Coverage--delivery identity and weighted
omission).} \emph{For each \(e\), the actual miss probability is}

\[m_e(S):=P(\operatorname{miss}_S(e))=1-\kappa_e(S)\delta_e(S).\]

\emph{For target weights \(w_e\ge0\) with \(W=\sum_ew_e>0\), define
\(\nu_e=w_e/W\). Then}

\[\mathcal M_\nu(S):=\sum_e\nu_em_e(S),\qquad \mathcal R_\nu(S):=1-\mathcal M_\nu(S)=\sum_e\nu_e\kappa_e(S)\delta_e(S).\]

\emph{These are weighted mean recall and expected omission, not
\(\inf_e P(D_S(e))\).}

\textbf{Proof.} Since \(D_S\subseteq C_S\),
\(P(D_S)=P(C_S)P(D_S\mid C_S)=\kappa_e\delta_e\). The weighted
identities follow by linearity. \(\blacksquare\)

If \(\{Z_i(e)\}_{i\in J_e}\) are mutually independent on a certified
subset \(J_e\subseteq S\), then

\[\kappa_e(S)\ge1-\prod_{i\in J_e}(1-p_i(e)),\]

Write \(P_J:=\prod_{i\in J_e}(1-p_i(e))\). If \(J_e=S\) and the full
family is independent, then \(\kappa_e(S)=1-P_J\) and the pointwise
decomposition is exactly

\[m_e(S)=P_J+(1-P_J)(1-\delta_e(S)).\]

For a proper certified independent subset \(J_e\subsetneq S\), the same
expression is generally only an upper bound:

\[m_e(S)=1-\kappa_e(S)\delta_e(S)\le P_J+(1-P_J)(1-\delta_e(S)),\]

because \(\kappa_e(S)\ge1-P_J\). In both cases the first term represents
independent-subset noncoverage and the second discounts delivery loss.
Perfect pass-through \(\delta_e=1\) is only a special case, not an
empirical default.

Without independence, the Fréchet bounds use only marginal coverage
probabilities:

\[\max\left\{0,1-\sum_{i\in S}p_i(e)\right\}\le P\!\left(\bigcap_{i\in S}Z_i(e)^c\right)\le1-\max_{i\in S}p_i(e).\]

Both bounds are sharp when only the marginals are known. In particular,
\(\kappa_e(S)\ge\max_{i\in S}p_i(e)\) requires no independence
certificate.

\subsubsection{6.2 Certifiable average bounds and a counterexample to
pooled
products}\label{certifiable-average-bounds-and-a-counterexample-to-pooled-products}

\textbf{Corollary 6.1 (Weighted lower recall certificates).} \emph{If
logs or a structural model provide
\(\kappa_e(S)\ge\underline\kappa_e(S)\ge0\) and
\(\delta_e(S)\ge\underline\delta_e(S)\ge0\), then}

\[\mathcal R_\nu(S)\ge\sum_e\nu_e\underline\kappa_e(S)\underline\delta_e(S),\qquad
\mathcal M_\nu(S)\le1-\sum_e\nu_e\underline\kappa_e(S)\underline\delta_e(S).\]

\emph{If \(\mathcal M_\nu(S)\le\varepsilon\), then for every
\(\eta\in(0,1]\), the weight of propositions with pointwise miss
probability above \(\eta\) satisfies}

\[\nu\{e:m_e(S)>\eta\}\le\varepsilon/\eta.\]

The first statement follows from Theorem 6.1 and monotonicity. The tail
statement is Markov's inequality for bounded \(m_e\). It limits the
weight of poorly covered propositions without pretending that every
proposition has high recall.

A pooled class mean cannot be inserted into a proposition-wise product.
Suppose two equally weighted propositions have class-coverages
\(p_i(e_1)=0.9\) and \(p_i(e_2)=0.1\) for two classes, with independence
within each proposition and perfect delivery. The correct weighted
omission is

\[\tfrac12(0.1)^2+\tfrac12(0.9)^2=0.410.\]

The pooled class mean is 0.5, but \((1-0.5)^2=0.250\), which would
overstate recall as 0.75 rather than the correct 0.59. Joint list
coverage is already a union outcome; exponentiating it again would also
count redundancy twice.

The former \((1-p_{\min})^{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}\) expression is therefore
not used as the main estimand. If a uniform pointwise theorem is
desired, it needs a genuine per-class, per-proposition floor and a
certified independent subset. Degenerate floors also invalidate the old
universal tightness statement: under its perfect-delivery assumption,
omission one almost surely forces every coverage indicator to vanish
almost surely; those degenerate indicators are mutually independent, so
an arbitrary smaller effective independent-subset size cannot be
constructed. The present Fréchet and weighted formulations avoid
assigning an empirical value to \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\) altogether.

\subsubsection{6.3 Two channels: failure-weighted recovery, not pooled
recovery}\label{two-channels-failure-weighted-recovery-not-pooled-recovery}

Let \(D_{\mathrm I}(e)\) and \(D_{\mathrm II}(e)\) denote actual
consumption by two channels, \(q_e=P(D_{\mathrm I}(e)^c)\), and, when
\(q_e>0\),

\[R_{\mathrm{II}}(e):=P(D_{\mathrm II}(e)\mid D_{\mathrm I}(e)^c).\]

When \(q_e=0\), omission is zero and the convention
\(q_e[1-R_{\mathrm{II}}(e)]=0\) is used without defining
\(R_{\mathrm{II}}(e)\).

\textbf{Proposition 6.1 (Total omission without cross-channel
independence).}

\[P(\operatorname{miss}(e))=q_e[1-R_{\mathrm{II}}(e)],\qquad
\mathcal M_\nu^{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_e\nu_eq_e[1-R_{\mathrm{II}}(e)].\]

If \(Q_\nu=\sum_e\nu_eq_e>0\) and
\(\bar R_{\mathrm{II},q}:=Q_\nu^{-1}\sum_e\nu_eq_eR_{\mathrm{II}}(e)\),
then

\[\mathcal M_\nu^{\mathrm{tot}}=(1-\bar R_{\mathrm{II},q})Q_\nu.\]

The recovery mean is weighted by Channel-I failure \(q_e\), not by all
opportunities and not by an unweighted pooled count. No independence
between channels is assumed. A pooled recovery computed on a selected
subpopulation does not identify a uniform per-proposition recovery
floor.

\subsubsection{6.4 An explicit availability--pipeline
decomposition}\label{an-explicit-availabilitypipeline-decomposition}

The conversion probability can be audited through three stages. Let
\(A_i(e)\) indicate authentic availability, \(L_i(e)\) successful
ingestion/authentication/linkage, and \(C_i(e)\) selection and actual
consumption, with \(D_i(e)=A_i(e)L_i(e)C_i(e)\). Define
availability-only omission \(M_A(e)=\cap_{i\in S}A_i(e)^c\) and total
omission \(M(e)=\cap_{i\in S}D_i(e)^c\). Then

\[M(e)=M_A(e)\;\dot\cup\;\big[M(e)\cap M_A(e)^c\big],\]

so

\[P(M(e))=P(M_A(e))+P\big(M(e)\cap M_A(e)^c\big).\]

The two terms are source-availability omission and loss after at least
one authentic item was available. A weighted sum gives the corresponding
\(\nu\)-decomposition. This makes the difficult engineering stages part
of the estimand rather than assuming them away with probability one. A
claim of zero filtering-induced omission applies only conditional on the
specified availability, linkage, and actual-consumption events; it is
not an unconditional zero-miss guarantee.

\subsubsection{6.5 What Clopper--Pearson can and cannot
certify}\label{what-clopperpearson-can-and-cannot-certify}

Freeze the monitoring set and the complete policy. Suppose \(n\)
independent target--environment--evaluation units are generated, with
target \(E_j\) sampled from \(\nu\) and with an independent realization
of the complete pipeline, including its environment, quotas, and
implementation randomness. Observe the \textbf{end-to-end} omission
indicators

\[X_j=\mathbf 1\{\text{no monitored channel actually consumes }E_j\},\qquad X=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j.\]

Under this independent-unit superpopulation model,
\(X_j\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mathcal M_\nu(S))\),
so \(X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,\mathcal M_\nu(S))\) regardless of
dependence among source classes within a proposition. Independent target
labels alone are insufficient when units share a day, quota, seed, or
global environment. Let \(U=\operatorname{CP}^+(X,n;\alpha)\) be the
one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper bound satisfying

\[\sum_{k=0}^{X}\binom nk U^k(1-U)^{n-k}=\alpha,\]

with \(U=1\) when \(X=n\). Then

\[\Pr\{\mathcal M_\nu(S)\le U\}\ge1-\alpha,\qquad
\Pr\{\mathcal R_\nu(S)\ge1-U\}\ge1-\alpha.\]

This statement estimates a weighted mean and automatically includes
delivery failures. It does not estimate \(\inf_{i,e}p_i(e)\) or
\(\inf_e m_e(S)\), and it requires no source-independence assumption.

By contrast, a CP interval for an average class-coverage proportion does
not yield a uniform proposition-conditional lower bound. Nor does it
identify a maximum independent-subset size, account for list selection
on the same data, or supply simultaneous confidence across classes and
propositions. A structural pointwise analysis would need fixed sampling
units for every \((e,i)\), a pre-frozen independence model, simultaneous
error allocation, and independent validation data. Time-replayed
propositions require independent time blocks or an explicit
superpopulation model. On one complete finite corpus, the proportion is
descriptive; it should not be presented as a future-probability
guarantee.

\subsubsection{6.6 Monitoring-portfolio
optimization}\label{monitoring-portfolio-optimization}

For realized coverage indicators \(Z_i(e,\omega)\), define

\[U_{\mathrm{mon}}(S)=\sum_e w_e\,\mathbb E\!\left[\max_{i\in S}Z_i(e,\omega)\right],\]

with the empty-set maximum equal to zero. Each realized max-coverage
function is monotone submodular, and expectation and nonnegative
weighting preserve both properties. No independence is needed for this
set-function result. Under a cardinality constraint, standard greedy
inherits the \(1-1/e\) ratio; under a knapsack constraint, the same
algorithm-specific guarantees reviewed in Section 5.4 apply (Nemhauser
et al., 1978; Leskovec et al., 2007; Sviridenko, 2004).

The independent-product expression is only one way to evaluate the
objective under a joint model. For positively correlated pairs it need
not be a conservative recall estimate, and for three or more classes
even pairwise positive correlation alone gives no universal one-sided
product interpretation. Monitoring selection and its evaluation should
therefore use independent validation data and the realized joint
coverage or a declared model, not a pooled proxy labeled as
\(p_{\min}\).

\subsection{7 Pull Operators, Budget Prices, and Fixed-Capacity
Choice}\label{pull-operators-budget-prices-and-fixed-capacity-choice}

\subsubsection{7.1 KKT prices with corner
cases}\label{kkt-prices-with-corner-cases}

Let \(x_u\ge0\) be minutes allocated to use \(u\), with differentiable,
increasing, concave values \(V_u\) and \(\sum_ux_u=T\). If
\(V(x)=\sum_uV_u(x_u)\), a feasible allocation \(x^*\) is optimal if and
only if some \(\nu\ge0\) satisfies

\[V_u'(x_u^*)=\nu\quad(x_u^*>0),\qquad V_u'(0)\le\nu\quad(x_u^*=0).\]

This is the standard concave KKT characterization. Funded uses share a
marginal value; unfunded uses need not. Activating a previously unfunded
use can produce first-order loss \((\nu-V_u'(0))\epsilon\), and a
universal second-order statement is false. Pull query time, push
reading, exploration, dwelling, and reflection can be compared through
this price only after converting all values to the same
utility-per-minute unit. Pandora's reservation index, a patch-cycle
average, a slot threshold, and a Whittle index are not automatically the
same scalar (Charnov, 1976; Weitzman, 1979; Whittle, 1988).

The KKT result does not solve interactions between retrieval and push
novelty, stochastic path-dependent opening costs, or global
knowledge-state coupling. Those remain outside the separable concave
model.

\subsubsection{7.2 Conditional EIG
submodularity}\label{conditional-eig-submodularity}

Let \(K\) be the current knowledge state and let
\(I_K(Q)=I(\omega;Y_Q\mid K)\) for a fixed nonadaptive query batch
\(Q\). For finite discrete variables, monotonicity follows from

\[I_K(Q\cup q)-I_K(Q)=I(Y_q;\omega\mid Y_Q,K)\ge0.\]

\textbf{Theorem 7.1 (Conditional-independence condition for EIG
submodularity).} \emph{If the full family \(\{Y_q:q\in\mathcal Q\}\) is
mutually conditionally independent given \((\omega,K)\), then \(I_K\) is
monotone submodular.}

\textbf{Proof.} For \(q\notin Q\),

\[\Delta(q\mid Q)=H(Y_q\mid Y_Q,K)-H(Y_q\mid\omega,K).\]

For \(Q'\subseteq Q\), conditioning reduces entropy, so
\(H(Y_q\mid Y_Q,K)\le H(Y_q\mid Y_{Q'},K)\) and
\(\Delta(q\mid Q)\le\Delta(q\mid Q')\). \(\blacksquare\)

Pairwise independence given only \(\omega\) is insufficient. If \(K\) is
fixed, it may be suppressed; if queries adaptively change \(K\), this
static batch result does not automatically apply. Standard greedy
guarantees are inherited only under the corresponding cardinality or
knapsack algorithms and certified domain.

\subsubsection{7.3 A correct fixed-capacity stochastic-choice
limit}\label{a-correct-fixed-capacity-stochastic-choice-limit}

Single-alternative rational-inattention logit is not a probability law
for overlapping events \(\{i\in A\}\). To model selection of exactly
\(m\) items, the action itself must be a subset. Let
\(\mathcal A_m=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=m\}\), and consider the
KL-regularized subset-choice problem

\[\max_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal A_m)}\left\{\sum_A p(A)U(A)-\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|\pi^0)\right\},\qquad \lambda>0,\]

where \(\pi^0\) is a positive reference distribution over subsets.
Equivalently, the objective is
\(\sum_Ap(A)U(A)+\lambda H(p)+\lambda\sum_Ap(A)\log\pi_A^0\).

\textbf{Proposition 7.2 (Subset logit and top-\(m\) limit).} \emph{The
unique optimum is}

\[p_\lambda(A)=\frac{\pi^0_A\exp(U(A)/\lambda)}{\sum_{B\in\mathcal A_m}\pi^0_B\exp(U(B)/\lambda)},\]

\emph{Inclusion probabilities are}

\[P_\lambda(i\in A)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_m:A\ni i}p_\lambda(A),\]

\emph{not a single-item logit proportional to
\(\pi_i^0e^{u_i/\lambda}\). As \(\lambda\downarrow0\), \(p_\lambda\)
concentrates on \(\arg\max_{A\in\mathcal A_m}U(A)\), weighted by
\(\pi^0\) if several optima exist. If \(U(A)=\sum_{i\in A}u_i\), this is
the top-\(m\) rule.}

\textbf{Proof.} The KKT conditions for maximizing the strictly concave
KL-regularized objective over the simplex give the exponential family
with reference weights \(\pi_A^0\). As \(\lambda\downarrow0\), exponents
dominated by the maximal set utility determine the limit.
\(\blacksquare\)

This proposition is a deliberate fixed-capacity subset model, not a
consequence of Matějka--McKay single-alternative choice. It also does
not identify the online opportunity-cost thresholds of Proposition 5.3
with a static information-cost parameter.

\subsection{8 Normative Extensions: Measurement, Override, and Public
Agenda}\label{normative-extensions-measurement-override-and-public-agenda}

\subsubsection{8.1 Measurement is not control
validation}\label{measurement-is-not-control-validation}

A three-dimensional state such as calibration, openness, and
intellectual humility is a modeling choice, not an identified latent
state from ten weekly prediction questions. Brier scores reflect
reliability, resolution, and uncertainty, and their proper-scoring
property is conditional on the participant's complete incentives.
Bounded observations on \([0,1]^3\) also do not justify a homoskedastic
Gaussian error model by default. A human measurement program needs
validated instruments, test--retest reliability, longitudinal
measurement-invariance checks, and a latent-variable model justified by
sample size. No convergence theorem for human ``virtue dynamics'' is
claimed in this paper.

The same limitation applies to LLM cohorts. Replaying a human paradigm
can test an AI mechanism, but it does not establish human transfer.
Model-specific Brier or persuasion results cannot identify human
learning, welfare, or deskilling without a predeclared, passed transfer
gate and ultimately direct human evidence.

\subsubsection{8.2 Emergency authorization is a finite design
table}\label{emergency-authorization-is-a-finite-design-table}

An emergency override can be restricted to a nonempty finite whitelist
\(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{safe}}\), a finite uncertainty set \(\Omega\), and
a declared loss table \(L(a,\omega)\). The standard minimax-regret
action is

\[a^*\in\arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{safe}}}\max_{\omega\in\Omega}\left[L(a,\omega)-\min_{a'\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{safe}}}L(a',\omega)\right].\]

Finite enumeration makes the value computable ex ante; this is Savage's
standard criterion, not a new regret theorem (Savage, 1951). A valid
implementation also needs explicit triggers, evidence authentication, a
time-feasibility check, rollback, logging, and post hoc review. If those
inputs are absent, the override is disabled rather than initialized with
unvalidated defaults. No dominance claim over every alternative policy
follows without specifying the loss model and audit costs.

\subsubsection{8.3 Agenda overlap is not common
knowledge}\label{agenda-overlap-is-not-common-knowledge}

For consumption sets \(C_i,C_j\), define the Jaccard agenda overlap
\(J(C_i,C_j)=|C_i\cap C_j|/|C_i\cup C_j|\). Suppose every member of a
group actually consumes the same public set \(S\), \(|S|=s=\gamma m\),
and each has a private part of size \(d=(1-\gamma)m\) disjoint from
\(S\). Then, for every pair,

\[J(C_i,C_j)\ge\frac{s}{s+2d}=\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}.\]

The proof is only set accounting: the intersection contains at least
\(S\) and the union has size at most \(s+2d\). The quantity is an
\textbf{agenda-overlap index}, not a ``fragmentation index'' whose
direction would be ambiguous. It does not require or establish truth,
attention beyond actual consumption, or common knowledge.

Common knowledge of an announcement requires a self-evident public
event: every member receives, attends to, and understands the same
authenticated announcement, and the public character of that receipt is
itself commonly observable. Private delivery receipts and ordinary audit
logs do not satisfy this condition merely because everyone knows the
mechanism. Even a genuine public announcement establishes common
knowledge of membership in the announced set, not common knowledge that
the proposition is true or important. Whether any real protocol achieves
those strong conditions is an empirical question left open.

\subsubsection{9.4 H8/H9: a failed or unadjudicated gate blocks human
extrapolation}\label{h8h9-a-failed-or-unadjudicated-gate-blocks-human-extrapolation}

The transfer rule is family-, version-, and task-specific. Every
required paradigm, direction, effect interval, sample minimum, and
failure action must be fixed before a family is used for human-side
conclusions. Any failed or unadjudicated component restricts that family
to AI-mechanism description.

The existing H8 results do not pass such a gate: opposing-exposure
directions reverse across families; accuracy-prompt effects are negative
across the three named families, but one is near zero and outside the
target effect interval; and one discrimination estimate exceeds the
stated reference interval. All H8 outcomes are AI-task descriptions
only, and the discrimination materials are not thereby certified as
memory-free. Data completion for named tags is not the same as transfer
validation.

H9 also cannot certify the gate as archived. On the agent's 40-question
task, O/C/Q/G Brier scores are 0.17046/0.23206/0.23306/0.28480, with
constant baseline 0.219375. O passes both the implemented Manifold-mean
\(\pm2\times\) rule and the separate 0.15--0.21 superforecaster
reference interval; G falls in the report's distinct 0.24--0.30
layperson interval; C/Q lie between those literature intervals, and
C/Q/G are worse than the agent task's constant baseline. The Manifold
anchor has Brier 0.11169 on different questions and base rates and uses
community terminal prices. These references are not one ``human band,''
do not implement the preregistered human-quantile criterion, and do not
test monotone correctability on the incompletely archived per-question
answers.

Accordingly, \textbf{no existing family is certified for human
transfer}, even though O passes two descriptive numerical criteria. All
replay findings in this paper are AI-task mechanism descriptions only.

\subsection{10 A Proposed Minimum Protocol for the Next Matched
Evaluation}\label{a-proposed-minimum-protocol-for-the-next-matched-evaluation}

A valid next experiment must start from raw candidate streams and
include S2d end-to-end LLM screening, S4 RAG with safety filtering, S5
welfare-oriented recommendation, and full pull/push/budget/monitoring
ablations. Arms must match model instance, raw inputs, retrieval
permissions, index snapshots, context and reasoning budgets, and actual
consumption limits. Family and backend cannot vary only with treatment.

At least three nonoverlapping time windows, two domains, and one
post-cutoff or private corpus are required, with a final locked corpus
untouched by design or tuning. Titles and authors should be anonymized
in the decontamination arm, with exact/normalized string and semantic
memory audits. Features require strict point-in-time timestamps. Ex-ante
decision-relevance labels should be elicited independently of later
citations and market outcomes; retrospective labels remain secondary
proxies.

The inferential unit is the independent corpus or month, not agent-day.
All numerators, denominators, missing records, conflicts, upstream
omissions, and run-level aggregation rules must be released. These are
design minima, not a guarantee that any completed implementation will
identify every structural effect. A time-stamped public registry entry
must precede unlocking the final corpus; an internally frozen document
is not called public preregistration. The full protocol and
missing/conflict rules are archived in
\texttt{docs/MINIMAL\_VALID\_EXPERIMENT\_v1.9.md}.

The future human protocol also needs one declared primary comparison and
a longitudinal power model. For orientation only, the normal
approximation at \(d=0.35\), two-sided \(\alpha=0.05\), and 80\% power
gives 128.145 analyzable participants per arm; 25\% attrition gives 171
randomized participants per arm, or 513 across three arms. Two
co-primary Bonferroni comparisons give approximately 207 per arm (621
total). Ceilings are applied independently to the unrounded \(n\) and
\(n/(1-\text{attrition})\), not sequentially to an already rounded
analyzable count. These are design calculations, not human data or a
substitute for variance estimates from a feasibility study.

\subsection{11 Limitations and
Conclusion}\label{limitations-and-conclusion}

The formal results are deliberately conditional. The update-value
identity does not validate a credibility estimate. The Gaussian
calculations do not describe every score distribution. Finite-pool
expectations do not determine any one realized ranking. Submodular
guarantees end at their certified ground sets and constraints. Weighted
recall depends on the declared target distribution, joint
source-coverage law, and actual delivery conversion; its mean confidence
interval is not a pointwise guarantee. KKT prices require separability
and concavity, EIG submodularity requires joint conditional independence
given \((\omega,K)\), and subset logit is not a theory of every
attention mechanism.

The empirical gaps are more consequential than the mathematical
restrictions. Existing direct baselines are unexecuted; academic and
financial scores contain retrospective information by construction;
upstream and shared-corpus dependence prevent independent-environment
interpretation; timestamp and model-memory audits are incomplete;
negative and interval-crossing results preclude a robust-benefit
narrative; and no human data or independent human mathematical sign-off
exists. A working source tree and locally frozen protocol are not an
immutable public release.

SEA nevertheless offers a useful discipline. It requires an information
system to state who owns the objective, how it may be changed, which
acquisition and filtering actions share a budget, what counts as a
target, and where a candidate was lost between publication and actual
consumption. Its near-term value is this explicit ownership, omission
accounting, and audit structure, together with negative results that
rule out overconfident deployment claims. Its comparative effectiveness
and human consequences remain hypotheses for the matched, temporally
valid experiments specified above.
